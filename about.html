{% include string-divider.html %} About

High-tech analog. Human-centered.

I'm Jacob Last, and I've been playing piano since I was five — I started university as a jazz piano major and ended up with a degree in mathematics. That combination turns out to be perfect preparation for this work: a piano is both a mechanical system and an expressive instrument, and caring for one well requires both analytical precision and sensitive listening.

I learned to tune the old-fashioned way: by ear — this is my gold standard, guided by a deep understanding of harmonic structure and acoustics. I also draw on the latest technology to achieve what I think of as high-tech analog: grounded in craft, informed by science. I value clear communication, respect for your home, and helping you feel more connected to your instrument.

My training is rooted in the aural tradition — I studied at the International Piano Technicians School and continue to deepen my skills through the Artisan School mentoring program, an ongoing apprenticeship under master technicians. I'm working toward the Registered Piano Technician (RPT) credential with the Piano Technicians Guild, the profession's primary standard of excellence.

I came to this work through someone who meant a great deal to me — a friend and early mentor who was one of the most generous and gifted people I've known. It was his example that first showed me what this profession could be at its finest: patient, skilled, and genuinely devoted to both the instrument and the people who play it. We lost him last year, and this work is, in part, my way of honoring everything he gave me.
